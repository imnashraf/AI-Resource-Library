The Problem Is Probably You

If you're always correcting Claude and going back and forth, the issue usually isn't the model, it's that you started building before you were aligned. You handed over a vague task, it made assumptions, and now you're fixing the gap one message at a time.

Delegating is different from correcting. When you delegate, you get the approach right up front, agree on it, then let Claude execute the whole thing without you hovering over every step. These four moves are how you make that switch. Get them right and you stop babysitting Claude and start handing it real work.

Move 01: Always Start in Plan Mode

Plan mode is a read-only state. Claude can read your files, search the codebase and ask you questions, but it can't change anything. It just thinks through the approach and hands you a proper plan to approve before any work begins.

  1. Turn it onIn the CLI, press Shift + Tab to cycle through the modes, default, then accept-edits, then plan. The current mode shows in the status bar. You can also launch straight into it.
claude --permission-mode plan
  1. Let it build the planDescribe the goal. Claude inspects the relevant files, weighs the options, flags risks and proposes the exact steps it will take. Nothing is touched yet.
  2. Approve, then it switches to buildingOnce the plan is right, approving it exits plan mode and Claude starts executing. Getting the plan right here is what saves you loads of back-and-forth later.
TipWant every session to open in plan mode? Set defaultMode to "plan" in your project's .claude/settings.json.

Move 02: Make Claude Ask You Questions

Plan mode already prompts a few questions on its own. But you can force the issue. Tell Claude to keep asking until it's almost certain what you want, and to make no assumptions. That one instruction kills most of your revision rounds before they happen.

Most bad output comes from a gap between what you meant and what Claude assumed. Closing that gap with questions up front is far cheaper than fixing it with corrections after the work is built. Paste a line like this at the start of your prompt:

Before you do anything, ask me clarifying questions
one at a time until you are 95% sure you know exactly
what I want. Make zero assumptions.

Why it works: Claude defaults to being helpful, which often means guessing and pressing on. Telling it to reach 95% certainty turns that instinct into questions instead of assumptions.

"Zero assumptions" is the key phrase: It's the line that stops Claude filling silent gaps with its best guess, the exact thing you end up correcting three messages later.

Pair it with plan mode: Questions in plan mode cost nothing, no files change while you align. By the time you approve, you're building the right thing the first time.

Move 03: Once Aligned, Bypass Permissions

By default Claude pauses for approval before each edit or command. That's the right safety net while you're unsure, but once you've planned everything out and you're fully aligned, those prompts are just friction. Bypass permissions lets Claude run the entire task without stopping to ask.

  1. Only after the plan is lockedThis works precisely because you did Moves 01 and 02 first. You're not skipping oversight, you front-loaded it into the plan, so there's nothing left to approve step by step.
  2. Switch it onUse the bypass-permissions mode, or launch with the flag below. Claude then executes every step of the agreed task uninterrupted and reports back when it's done.
claude --dangerously-skip-permissions
Use It WiselyThe official guidance is to use full bypass in a trusted or isolated setup, a project where a bad command can't do lasting damage. Your deny rules still apply even in this mode. If you want most of the speed with a safety check, Claude Code's newer Auto mode runs without routine prompts while a classifier reviews actions before they execute.

Move 04: Keep Updating Your CLAUDE.md File

CLAUDE.md is a plain markdown file Claude Code loads at the start of every session. It's your project's permanent memory, conventions, preferences, the things you'd otherwise re-explain every time. The better that file is, the less back-and-forth you do.

  1. Tell Claude to update itThe moment you learn something or set a new preference, don't just remember it, bank it. One sentence does the job.
Add that to CLAUDE.md so you remember it next time.
  1. Keep it under 200 linesCLAUDE.md loads into context every session, so a bloated file eats the room Claude needs to actually work. Anthropic's own guidance is to target under 200 lines, past that, adherence to your rules drops.
  2. Prune the old stuffGo in regularly and clear out anything stale or no longer true. A short, dense file where every line earns its place beats a long one every time.
NoteClaude Code also keeps its own auto-memory, quietly noting patterns it picks up as it works. CLAUDE.md is the part you control, the rules and preferences you want applied every single session.

You're Not Correcting. You're Delegating.

Get these four right and the relationship flips. You stop babysitting Claude through every edit and start handing it whole tasks. Plan it once, set the rules, let it run.

  • 03 Bypass permissions, once aligned, let it run the whole task uninterrupted.
