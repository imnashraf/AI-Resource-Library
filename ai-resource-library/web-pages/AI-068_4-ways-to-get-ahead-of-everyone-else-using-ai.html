The four systems worth building before everyone else catches on: a second brain, one real workflow, a specialised AI teammate, and one automation that runs itself.

Before you start

Every setup in this guide is written for both Claude and ChatGPT. Projects, custom instructions and memory are available on free accounts in a limited form; a full knowledge base and scheduled automations need a paid plan on either platform. Setup details were checked against Anthropic and OpenAI documentation in August 2026.

Way 01 · Claude & ChatGPT: Build An AI Second Brain

Tell it everything about you, your business and exactly how you like to work, so it never has to ask you for context again.

Most people start every chat from zero, re-explaining who they are, what their business does and how they like things done. A second brain removes that entirely. Every conversation starts already briefed.

What this looks like

  • Instructions: What it should always know about you and how you work, written once.
  • Knowledge: The actual documents: your offer, your numbers, your past writing, your rules.
  • Memory: What it keeps learning automatically as you use it, without you curating it.
  • Your business stage, goals and current numbers
  • Your own voice: past scripts, posts, emails you'd stand behind
  • Standing rules: how you like feedback, formatting, what never to do

Way 01 Setup: Build An AI Second Brain

Claude

Setup: New Project → Set project instructions → add to Knowledge

Setup detail: Custom instructions, project knowledge

Needs: Any plan for basics, paid plan for a full knowledge base

ChatGPT

Setup: Settings → Personalization → Custom instructions, plus a Project

Setup detail: Custom instructions (global) and project instructions

Needs: Any plan, more room on paid plans

Note

Turn memory on properly, it's not automatic. In Claude, go to Settings > Memory and switch on both Search and reference chats and Generate memory from chat history; it's rolling out plan by plan so it may not have reached your account yet. In ChatGPT, go to Settings > Personalization > Memory and enable Saved memories and Reference chat history separately.

Paste this in (Claude)

You are working inside my second brain. Use everything below
as context for every conversation in this project.

WHO I AM
[Name, role, background in two or three lines]

MY BUSINESS
[What it does, what stage it's at, current goals, current
numbers if relevant]

HOW I LIKE TO WORK
[Tone, level of detail, what to push back on versus accept,
anything you should never do]

Never ask me to re-explain any of this. If a document I've
added contradicts what's here, flag it and ask which is
current rather than guessing.

Paste this in (ChatGPT)

Here is what I want you to know about me in every
conversation:

WHO I AM: [name, role, background]
MY BUSINESS: [what it does, stage, goals, numbers if relevant]
HOW I WORK: [tone, detail level, what to challenge, what to avoid]

Apply this by default. Don't make me repeat it. If something I
upload later contradicts this, ask me which is current.

Way 02 · Claude & ChatGPT: Build One AI Workflow

Don't automate one small task. Automate the entire process, start to finish, in a single run.

Most people use Claude or ChatGPT one message at a time: ask a question, get an answer, ask the next one. A workflow strings the whole process together so one prompt produces the finished thing, not just a piece of it.

What this looks like

  • Map the stages: Write down the steps you'd actually do yourself, in order.
  • Chain them together: Each stage's output becomes the next stage's input, in one prompt.
  • Run it inside your second brain: So it already knows your voice, your business, your rules.
  • Research trending topics, write the script in your voice, output filming and editing notes
  • Pull last week's numbers, write the summary, draft the message to your team
  • Read a transcript, extract the decisions, draft the follow up emails

Way 02 Setup: Build One AI Workflow

Claude

Setup: Run inside your second brain project, or save as a Scheduled task

Setup detail: Web search on for research stages

Needs: Web search enabled

ChatGPT

Setup: Run inside your project, or save as a Scheduled task

Setup detail: Web browsing on

Needs: Browsing enabled

Note

Real time trend research is only ever a starting point, not a fact check. Treat Stage 1 as a shortlist to sense check yourself, not a guarantee that a topic is actually trending right now.

Paste this in (Claude)

Run this as one workflow, in order. Do not stop or
summarise between stages, do all three in the same response.

STAGE 1: RESEARCH
Search for what's trending in [your niche] right now. Give me
the five most relevant topics, with one line on why each one
is moving.

STAGE 2: SCRIPT
Pick the strongest topic from Stage 1. Write it as a script in
my voice, [length], with a hook in the first line, using the
tone and rules from my project instructions.

STAGE 3: PRODUCTION NOTES
For the script above, give me filming notes (shots, pacing, on
screen text) and editing notes (cuts, sound, captions).

Paste this in (ChatGPT)

Do this as one workflow. Complete all three stages in
the same response, don't stop in between.

1. Search for what's trending in [your niche] right now. Give
me the five most relevant topics and why each is moving.
2. Pick the strongest one and write it as a script in my voice,
[length], hook in the first line.
3. Give me filming notes and editing notes for that script.

Way 03 · Claude & ChatGPT: Build An AI Cofounder Or Teammate

Stop going back and forth over chat. Let it do real work. Give it one specific job and let it become insanely good at it.

A general assistant that helps with everything ends up mediocre at all of it. A teammate with one job, fed everything that job needs, starts producing finished work instead of draft after draft you have to fix.

What this looks like

  • Give it one job, not ten: A narrow, specific role beats a general assistant every time.
  • Feed it only what that job needs: A focused knowledge base, not your entire business.
  • Hand it a brief, not a conversation: Ask for the finished thing, then review it. Don't co-write it line by line.
  • A content lead that only writes scripts in your voice
  • An ops lead that only handles weekly reporting
  • A research analyst that only tracks your competitors

Way 03 Setup: Build An AI Cofounder Or Teammate

Claude

Setup: A separate Project for each role, not one shared project

Setup detail: Project instructions define the job and the authority to finish it

Needs: Paid plan for a real knowledge base per role

ChatGPT

Setup: A separate Project for each role

Setup detail: Project-only memory so roles don't bleed into each other

Needs: Any plan, project-only memory on paid plans

Honest limit

This still isn't a person. It can produce the finished draft, the plan, the report, but it can't act outside the chat, send the email or post the content, unless you separately connect a tool that lets it. Treat it as a teammate who finishes the work, and you're still the one who ships it.

Paste this in (Claude)

Your one job in this project is [specific function, e.g.
writing my weekly newsletter].

You are not here to brainstorm or hand me options. Research,
draft and finish the work end to end, using everything in this
project's knowledge. Give me a finished piece, formatted and
ready to use, not a plan for one.

Only ask me a question if something in the brief is genuinely
missing. Never ask just to check in on style or tone, that's
already defined in this project.

Paste this in (ChatGPT)

Your only job in this project is [specific function].

Don't give me options or ask what I'd prefer, that's defined
here already. Research it, draft it and finish it, using only
what's in this project. Hand me the finished output, ready to
use.

Ask only if something you genuinely need is missing from this
project's files.

Way 04 · Claude & ChatGPT: Build One Automation

Choose a task you want handled every single week, without ever having to open Claude or ChatGPT.

Everything above still needs you to open the app. An automation doesn't. You pick one recurring task, put it on a schedule, and it's simply done by the time you'd have gotten to it.

What this looks like

  • Pick one task: The one that costs you the most time on the same day every week.
  • Save it as a prompt: Everything it needs to do the job, written once.
  • Put it on a schedule: Same prompt, run automatically, no need to open anything.
  • A morning briefing: what's on today, what needs a reply, what's due
  • A weekly expenditure report: what came in, what went out, what's left
  • A competitor check: what changed, what they're doing differently, what to react to

Way 04 Setup: Build One Automation

Claude

Setup: Scheduled → New task → Setup manually

Setup detail: Weekly, choose the day and time

Needs: Cowork, available on Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise

ChatGPT

Setup: Scheduled → New task

Setup detail: Weekly, choose the day and time

Needs: Plus, Business, Pro or Enterprise

Note

Neither platform fires on an event, both run on a timer. Weekly is the right cadence for reporting tasks like this one; save the daily cadence for things you genuinely need every morning, like a briefing, so you're not tuning it out by Wednesday.

Paste this in (Claude)

Research my top three competitors: [name them]. Check
their pricing page, anything they've published in the last
seven days, and any product or feature announcements.

Tell me: what changed since last week, anywhere they're pricing
or positioning differently, and one thing genuinely worth
reacting to. If nothing meaningful changed, say so plainly
rather than padding it out.

Keep it to one page.

Paste this in (ChatGPT)

Every week, check my top three competitors: [name them].
Look at their pricing page, anything published in the last
seven days, and any product announcements.

Tell me what changed since last week, any pricing or
positioning shift, and one thing worth reacting to. If nothing
meaningful changed, say that directly rather than inventing a
takeaway. One page, no padding.

Closing: Four Systems. Build Them In Order.

None of this needs code, an API key or a developer. It needs one project, written properly, and the discipline to keep feeding it. Everyone has access to the same tools right now. Almost nobody sets them up like this.

  • Start with the second brain: One project, your instructions, your knowledge. Everything else gets better once this exists.
  • Then build one workflow: Not ten. One real process, chained end to end, that you actually run every week.
  • Then specialise one teammate: Give it one job and let it get good at exactly that, nothing else.
  • Then automate one task: Whichever one costs you the most time on the same day, every week.
