Chapter 01, Context

DON'T, Don't start every chat from a cold start. Every new conversation with no context starts from zero. Claude has no idea who you are, what your business does, or how you want things written. You end up re-explaining yourself every single time.

DO, Build Projects for everything you do regularly. A Project is a persistent workspace with its own instructions and uploaded files. I have separate ones for content, client work, product, and admin. Each one already knows the context before I type a word. The output quality difference is night and day.

DO, Upload your knowledge files into each Project. Brand guide. SOPs. Examples of writing you like. Pricing. Claude reads them when relevant. The more it has, the less you have to explain.

Chapter 02, Instructions

DON'T, Don't give Claude vague instructions. "Write in a casual tone" is useless. It could mean anything. You'll get generic output and blame the model.

DO, Write specific rules. Sentence length limits. Banned phrases. British English. Real examples of writing you like, pasted straight in. "Write like you're telling a mate something you found, short sentences, no hype" beats "be casual" every time.

DO, Tell Claude what NOT to do. Banning patterns is often stronger than describing what you want. "Never use em dashes. Never open with 'In today's world'. No bullet points unless I ask." That single instruction removes most of the obvious AI tells.

Chapter 03, Memory

DON'T, Don't wait for Claude to figure you out passively. Memory works in the background and learns over time. But if you leave it to chance, it takes ages to build a useful picture of you.

DO, Teach it directly. "Remember I run an AI agency in Manchester." "Remember I prefer British English." Say "remember this" and it stores it. Say "forget this" and it drops it. You're in control.

DO, Set your User Preferences. A short paragraph in Settings about how you want Claude to behave. Tone, formatting, expertise level. It applies everywhere, across every chat. Memory is global. Project instructions are specific. Use both.

Chapter 04, Skills

DON'T, Don't re-explain your process every time. If you find yourself pasting the same framework or instructions into chat after chat, you're wasting time Claude could be saving you.

DO, Install Skills and build your own. A Skill is a packaged set of instructions that teaches Claude how to handle a specific task. Teach it once, benefit every time. The five worth installing now: frontend design, document creation (docx), PDF, spreadsheets (xlsx), presentations (pptx). The output jump on each is immediate.

DO, Add to them constantly. Every time Claude gets something slightly wrong, feed the fix back into the Skill as a rule. Specific in, specific out. That's the whole game.

Chapter 05, Connectors

DON'T, Don't treat Claude as a chatbot that lives in a tab. Copying information between Claude and your other apps by hand defeats the point. That's manual work you've just moved, not removed.

DO, Connect it to your actual tools. MCP connectors plug Claude into Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Notion, Slack, and more. Once connected it reads your real data and takes action inside those tools. "Check my calendar for tomorrow." "Draft a reply to that email." It handles the coordination.

DO, Know the boundary. Claude only uses connectors when you ask. It's not watching your inbox. And on Gmail it drafts but doesn't send. You approve every send. That's deliberate.

Chapter 06, Decisions

DON'T, Don't let Claude just agree with you. Claude is trained to be helpful. The more confident you sound, the more it agrees. Push back and it folds. Helpful and honest are not the same thing, and most people are getting reassurance dressed up as advice.

DO, Run the LLM Council on big calls. Five advisors, different thinking styles, same question. Contrarian, First Principles, Expansionist, Outsider, Executor. Then they peer review each other blind. Then a chairman gives you one clear recommendation. Use it for pricing, hiring, positioning, anything you can't easily reverse. Skip it for quick lookups and email drafts.

DO, Notice the signal. If you're re-asking Claude the same question hoping for a different answer, stop. Open a new chat. Run the Council instead.

Chapter 07, Automation

DON'T, Don't draft repetitive work by hand. Lead replies. Meeting recaps. Weekly reports. If you're doing the same shaped task over and over, you're the bottleneck, not the work.

DO, Build scheduled tasks in Claude Code. A task that checks Gmail hourly, drafts a reply to each new lead in your voice, and saves it to drafts. Thirty seconds per lead to review and send. A Monday morning task that pulls your data, writes the client report, and drafts the email before you open your laptop. I get roughly two working days back a week from three of these.

DO, Budget the tuning week. Week one the drafts sound slightly off. That's normal. Edit them, note why, feed the reason back into the file. By week three it sounds like you wrote it. Nothing good in AI automation works perfectly on day one. Build it, break it, fix it, tune it.

Chapter 08, Plans and Models

DON'T, Don't assume you need to spend big to start. Most of this works on the free plan. You don't need to pay anything to test Projects, Memory, and connectors.

DO, Upgrade when you hit the limit, not before. Pro is around $20 a month and unlocks Projects, the latest models, and far more messages. If you're using Claude for real work it pays for itself in the first week. Once your automations run at volume you may outgrow it. Max comes in two tiers, $100 (5x usage) and $200 (20x). Move up only when Pro genuinely starts stalling you.

DO, Use the strongest model for the job. Use the latest Opus for hard reasoning, long documents, and anything where the writing has to land. Lighter models are fine for routine drafting and data pulls inside automations. Match the model to the task.

Chapter 09, The Mindset

DON'T, Don't use Claude as a toy. Asking it for the odd answer here and there is fine. But that's a fraction of what it does, and it's the difference between someone who plays with AI and someone who runs on it.

DO, Treat it as infrastructure. Projects for context. Memory for preferences. Skills for process. Connectors for action. Council for decisions. Scheduled tasks for the repetitive stuff. Set up properly, Claude stops being a thing you open and becomes the layer your work runs through.

The full stack: Projects · Memory · Skills · Connectors · LLM Council · Scheduled Tasks. Each one multiplies the others. None of them are hard to set up. All of them are ignored by most people.
