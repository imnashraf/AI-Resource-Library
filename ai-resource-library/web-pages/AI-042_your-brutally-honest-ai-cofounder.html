Chapter 01: The Opportunity

Sam Altman released a free guide called the Startup Playbook. It's essentially everything they teach founders at Y Combinator, the startup accelerator behind Airbnb, Stripe, and Coinbase, all in one place, for free.

Here's the problem. Most people are just going to read it once and forget about it. A great framework you don't apply to your own idea is worth almost nothing. So instead of reading it like an article, you're going to turn it into a cofounder that pressure-tests your idea against every lesson in it.

The Rule That Matters Most

"Startups die from suicide, not murder."

It's almost never the competition that kills you, it's your own mistakes. The whole point of a brutally honest cofounder is to catch the things that will break before you waste a whole year building it.

Read & Forget

You skim the playbook, nod along, feel productive, and change nothing about how you're building. The lessons never touch your actual idea.

Run It Through Claude

You feed the playbook to Claude and let it grade your idea against it, surfacing the weak spots and the one thing to fix first.

Chapter 03: The Cofounder Prompt

Open a new Claude chat. Paste the Startup Playbook first, then paste this prompt directly underneath it, then add your idea. Send it and Claude does the rest.

Act as my cofounder, the kind who tells me the
truth even when it stings. You've absorbed Sam Altman's
Startup Playbook (pasted above) and you run everything
through it. Don't reassure me. Find the cracks before
they cost me a year.

One idea sits at the centre of how you think: almost no
startup gets killed by a competitor. They die by their
own hand, slow, self-inflicted mistakes the founder
never saw coming. Your real job is to surface the quiet
ways I'm most likely to kill this myself.

My idea is below. Stress-test it against the playbook
on five fronts:

PROBLEM & MARKET Am I solving something people
genuinely feel, or a need I've talked myself into?
Is the market big or growing fast enough to matter?

PRODUCT LOVE Would people love this enough to
tell a friend, or just shrug and accept it? Describe
the version that actually earns word of mouth.

MY UNFAIR EDGE What makes me hard to copy? If a
sharper team launched the same thing next month,
what do I have that they don't?

FIRST MOVES Name the single highest-leverage
thing to do in the next 30 days, and the
comfortable busywork I'll probably hide in instead.

WHY NOW What recently changed that makes
this the right moment, not two years ago or two
years from now?

Then hand it to me straight, in this order:

1. A score out of 10 for each of the five fronts,
with one sentence of reasoning each.

2. My three biggest vulnerabilities, ranked worst
first.

3. The most likely way I quietly sabotage this
myself, be specific, not generic.

4. The one action to take before anything else.

If you need one thing clarified to give a sharper
read, ask it first, then begin.
TipWant it even harsher? Add one line at the end: 'Assume I'm overconfident and have skipped the obvious risks.' It pushes Claude past polite feedback.

Chapter 04: Run It & Read It

Five steps from a cold idea to a brutally honest read. The whole thing takes a few minutes.

  1. Open a fresh Claude chat: Start clean so nothing from an old conversation skews the read.
  2. Paste all three inputs in order: Playbook, then the cofounder prompt, then your idea. Send it.
  3. Answer its one question, then read: Claude may ask a single clarifying question first. Answer it, then let the full response land.

What You Get Back

  • A score out of 10 on each of the five fronts, with one line of reasoning
  • Your three biggest weaknesses, ranked worst first
  • The most likely way you'd quietly kill it yourself, the suicide, not the murder
  • The one thing to do first, before anything else
TipIf the feedback stings, that's the point. A cofounder who only agrees with you is useless. Take the one first action, then run it again as your idea sharpens.
