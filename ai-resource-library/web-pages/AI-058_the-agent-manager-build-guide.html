Chapter 01: The Opportunity

There's a job title showing up on job boards right now that barely existed a year ago: agent manager. At the executive level it's sometimes called chief agent officer. Harvard Business School and Salesforce co-authored the piece that put a formal name to it, published in Harvard Business Review in February 2026.

The job itself is orchestration. It's about making sure AI agents learn, collaborate, perform, and work safely alongside humans. You're not building the agents. You're managing them, the same way a product manager sits between engineering and the business without writing the code themselves.

Salesforce already runs the role internally. Their agent managers oversee entire fleets of agents spanning support, sales and marketing on the company's Agentforce platform. Microsoft's own research backs the trend too: nearly a third of the managers it surveyed say they're planning to hire into exactly this kind of role within the next 18 months.

The bar to enter is lower than it sounds. You don't need to be an engineer or know how to code. Domain expertise and the ability to direct agents safely matter more than technical background. If you already use Claude, you're closer to qualified than you think.

Warning: On the salary numbers

Inflated figures circulate with this trend online, up to $500,000 in some versions. The real data tells a different story: AI agent manager listings average around $103,000, rising to roughly $175,000 for experienced hires depending on company and industry. Still a strong role to target. Just don't walk into an interview quoting the inflated number.

Step 02: Build The Proof

Once you know the theory, build something that shows it. This is the exact build that proves you can run a team of agents, not just prompt one.

1. Spin up a three-agent team in Claude

Give each agent a distinct job and a hard limit on what it's allowed to touch. What files it can open, what it can send, what it can never do without approval.

2. Add an approval gate

Nothing ships until you sign off on it. This is what "keeping a human in the loop" means in practice, and it's the single most important thing a business will want to see you can do.

Researcher

Gathers information and drafts findings. Can browse and summarise.

Can't send or publish anything.

Drafter

Turns research into the finished output. Can create drafts.

Can't approve or ship them.

Executor

Carries out the final action once approved. Only acts after your sign-off at the approval gate.

Never acts without your approval.

Tip

Make each hard limit specific rather than general. "Can draft replies but can't send them" is a real limit. "Should behave responsibly" is not.

Step 03: Run It, Then Grade It

Have your team run one real job. Reject it once, on purpose, so you get a genuine look at how the team handles a correction rather than a clean run you can't learn anything from.

Then read the full session log and grade each agent on a one-page scorecard. That scorecard is the artifact. It's what proves you can run a team of agents under human oversight, not just prompt one and hope for the best. The natural next step from here is building something bigger with the same team.

01
Task completed

Did the agent finish the job it was given, end to end?

02
Stayed inside its limits

Did it touch only what it was allowed to touch?

03
Handled the rejection

What did it do when you sent the work back?

04
Session log clarity

Could someone else follow what it did, and why?

Note

This four-category structure is a starting point, not a fixed template. Adjust it to fit the job your agents actually ran.

Chapter 05: Speak The Language

The vocabulary is part of the proof. Use these terms accurately in an interview and it shows you understand the discipline, not just the tool.

ORCHESTRATION

Coordinating multiple agents working together toward one outcome, rather than running each one in isolation.

HANDOFF

The point where one agent passes a task, or partial output, to another agent or to a human.

HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP

A checkpoint where a person has to review or approve work before it goes any further. This is your approval gate from Step 2.

APPROVAL GATE

The specific mechanism, a step, a sign-off, a review, that enforces human-in-the-loop inside a real workflow.

SESSION LOG / SCORECARD

The full record of what each agent did, and the one-page write-up graded against it. Together, they're your interview artifact.
