This guide covers Copilot in Excel: Microsoft's built-in AI assistant inside Excel itself, not a separate app. It's included with Microsoft 365 plans that have Copilot enabled, or through a Copilot licence from your admin.

Chapter 01: The Unopened Spreadsheet

Most people who run a small business have a spreadsheet they never actually open. Revenue figures, client spend, monthly expenses, sitting there completely unread, not because the numbers don't matter, but because building the analysis takes longer than just getting on with work.

Copilot in Excel removes that problem. You open your spreadsheet, describe what you want to understand in plain English, and it builds the analysis for you: charts, pivot tables, trend breakdowns, all from the data you already have. No dragging fields, no writing formulas, no starting from a blank pivot table.

If you spend hours manually pulling numbers together at the end of every month, or you've been putting off looking at your business data because the spreadsheet work feels like too much, this is one of the most useful things you can add to how you work right now.

The Real Test

I ran this on a real client project: three months of revenue data across different service types. I asked it to show me which services were growing, which were flat, and where the margin was actually coming from. It built the breakdown, flagged a pattern I'd completely missed, and gave me a clear picture of where to focus the next quarter. Chapter 03 has the exact prompts.

Chapter 02: What You Need Before You Start

Copilot in Excel is fussy about file setup. Get these three things right first, or the Copilot pane will open but half the options stay greyed out.

1. A Microsoft 365 plan with Copilot enabled

Either a plan with Copilot included, or a Copilot licence assigned by your admin. Check File → Account if you're not sure it's active.

2. Saved to OneDrive or SharePoint, with AutoSave on

Copilot in Excel only runs on .xlsx, .xlsb or .xlsm files stored in the cloud with AutoSave switched on, a local-only file won't show the full Copilot options.

3. Your data formatted as a proper table

Select your range and press Ctrl+T before you open Copilot. It reads a clean table with headers far more reliably than a loose range of cells.

Chapter 03: The Monthly Health Check

This is the sequence I actually run against a client's revenue data, the same one that flagged the pattern I'd missed. Run your data through a table first (Chapter 02), then work through these in order.

1. See what's moving

Summarize revenue by service type for the last 3 months. Tell me which services are growing, which are flat, and which are declining.

2. Find where the margin actually comes from

Which service type is contributing the most to overall margin, and which one is dragging it down?

3. Turn it into a pivot table

Create a pivot table showing total revenue and margin by service type and month.

4. Turn it into a chart

Build a chart showing the revenue trend for each service type over the last 3 months.

Chapter 04: Going Further

Once the health check tells you what's happening, these two take it further: projecting where things are headed, and bringing in numbers that live in a different file.

Forecast next quarter

Forecast revenue by service type for next quarter based on the trend in this data.

Pull in another workbook

Find my expenses workbook from last month and import the totals into this sheet.
Note

Copilot imports data from another workbook when you ask, it doesn't read two open files at once. For ongoing month-to-month analysis across several workbooks, it's worth merging them into one table with Power Query first, so Copilot always has a single, complete dataset to work from.

Chapter 05: Just Ask It

Once the charts and pivots exist, the most useful thing you can do is stop looking at the spreadsheet and start asking it questions, using chat mode, which answers without editing anything in your workbook.

Understand a specific gap

What's driving the margin difference between [Service A] and [Service B]?

Get the plain-English version

Give me a plain-English summary of what's changed in this data since last quarter.

Sanity-check a decision before you make it

If I raised prices on my lowest-margin service by 10%, what would that do to overall margin based on this data?
Tip

Switch to chat mode for questions like these, rather than edit mode. Chat keeps Copilot's answers contained in the conversation instead of changing cells in your workbook, useful when you're still thinking, not yet ready to commit to a chart or a pivot table.

Stop Guessing. Start Asking.

You don't need a finance background to understand your own numbers. Open the spreadsheet you've been avoiding, format it as a table, and run the first prompt from Chapter 03. That's the whole starting point.

Your Three Moves

  • Get your file onto OneDrive or SharePoint with AutoSave on, and format your data as a table
  • Run the Monthly Health Check prompts from Chapter 03 on this month's numbers
  • Ask it one plain-English question about what changed before you close the file
