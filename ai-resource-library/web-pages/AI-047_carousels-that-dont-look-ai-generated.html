What Claude Design Is

Claude Design is Anthropic's canvas-based design tool, launched in April 2026 and powered by the Opus 4.7 vision model. It is not the chat box. It has its own canvas where you describe what you want, Claude builds a first version, and you refine it visually, dragging, restyling and editing single elements without breaking the rest.

That is the shift the video is talking about. Asking the chat to "write carousel copy" just gives you words you then have to rebuild in Canva. Claude Design produces the actual slides, layout, typography, hierarchy, the lot, as an editable design you can export. One input, a finished set.

The honest version of "in minutes"

The first draft really does land in a couple of minutes. A carousel you'd actually post takes a refine pass or two on top, call it 10 to 15 minutes for a polished set, not two. Still a fraction of the hours it used to take, which is the whole point.

Why AI Carousels Look AI

Before the fix, name the problem. There are six tells that give an AI carousel away, and they all come from the same place: vague prompts. Ask for "clean, modern, professional" and the model pulls toward the median of everything it has seen. The median is the AI look.

01
The default font
Inter, Roboto, system sans. Anthropic's own design guidelines literally tell Claude to avoid these, they're the typographic fingerprint of AI output.
02
Four cards in a grid
Stacked, evenly-sized boxes with no focal point. The layout every AI tool reaches for when you don't tell it otherwise.
03
The purple-to-cyan gradient
The universal "AI made this" background. If your slides fade from violet to teal, people clock it instantly.
04
Perfect symmetry
Everything centred, evenly spaced, no tension. Real design has a hierarchy and a focal point, AI defaults to bland balance.
05
No narrative
A topic chopped into slides instead of a story. Good carousels pull you through: hook, then tension, then payoff.
06
Generic everything
Stock icons, median spacing, nothing you remember. Designed by committee to be vaguely nice, so it gets scrolled past.
The one fix behind all six

Stop describing, start referencing. Swap "modern and clean" for a real aesthetic, exact fonts and exact colours. Specific inputs can't collapse to the median, that's the entire trick to the next three chapters.

The Setup: Topic, Style, Brand

Three inputs decide whether your carousel looks designed or generated. Get these right and the prompt in the next chapter does the rest.

TOPIC

Narrow and outcome-led

One audience, one promise. "5 ChatGPT prompts that save estate agents an hour a day" beats "AI tips". The tighter the audience, the sharper every slide gets.

STYLE

Reference, don't describe

Name a real aesthetic: an editorial print magazine, Swiss typographic, a brand you admire. Pick a distinctive type pairing (a characterful display face plus a clean body face) and hand over an exact palette of 2 to 3 hex codes. Ban Inter, Roboto, Arial and any purple gradient outright.

BRAND

Make the set feel like one designer made it

Your handle small in the same corner on every slide. Same margins, same type scale, same accent right across the set. If you already have a design system, Claude Design can read it and build to it.

The structure, every time

Slide 1 is a hook (under 8 words). Every middle slide carries one idea: a short headline and a line or two, never a wall of text. The last slide is the call to action. Hook, one idea per slide, CTA.

The Full Prompt

Start with the quick version, this is the "topic and a style" the video describes, with just enough guard-rails to dodge the AI look. Paste it straight into Claude Design and swap the brackets.

THE QUICK PROMPT
Build me a 7-slide Instagram carousel about [topic] for [audience].
Style: [reference, e.g. bold editorial print, dark mode, one big
idea per slide]. Format 4:5, 1080 x 1350px.
Slide 1 is a hook under 8 words. Last slide is a CTA to [action].
Don't use Inter or Roboto. No gradient backgrounds. One focal
point per slide with generous negative space.

It works because every line is doing a job: a narrow topic, a referenced style, an exact format, a hook-and-CTA structure, and three explicit bans that kill the most common tells. When you want full control, exact fonts, exact palette, a locked system across the set, use the full prompt below.

Tip

Drag in a screenshot of a carousel you love before you send. Claude Design reads images, so a reference beats any description, it'll match the structure and feel instead of guessing.

The Full Carousel Prompt

PASTE AND SWAP THE BRACKETS
I want a [number]-slide Instagram carousel.

TOPIC: [topic], for [specific audience] who want to [outcome].

FORMAT
- 4:5 portrait, 1080 x 1350px per slide, [number] slides total.
- Slide 1 is the hook. The last slide is the CTA.

STRUCTURE (one idea per slide)
- Slide 1 - Hook: a bold claim, a sharp number or an open loop.
  Under 8 words on the slide.
- Middle slides - one single idea each: a short headline plus one
  or two supporting lines. Never more than that.
- Final slide - CTA: tell them exactly what to do ([your CTA]).

DESIGN (this is the part that matters)
- Style reference: design it like [a real reference, e.g. an
  editorial print magazine, Swiss typographic, a premium fintech
  brand]. Do NOT default to generic SaaS.
- Type: do NOT use Inter, Roboto, Arial or Space Grotesk. Pair one
  characterful display face for headlines with one clean body face.
- Palette: use exactly these colours, [2-3 hex codes]. No purple-
  to-cyan gradients.
- One visual anchor per slide. Big type, generous negative space.
  Not four cards in a grid.
- Vary where the headline sits across slides so the set feels
  designed, not templated, but keep one consistent system:
  same margins, same type scale, same accent on every slide.
- Put [@yourhandle] small in the same corner of every slide.

Build all [number] slides on the canvas, then I'll refine.
Why it beats the AI look

Every tell from Chapter 02 has a matching instruction here: a referenced style, banned fonts, an exact palette, one anchor per slide, and a locked system across the set. You're not asking for "good design", you're removing the room it has to be generic.

Refine and Export

The first draft is a starting point, not the post. Two or three plain-English tweaks take it from "AI built this" to "a designer built this". Talk to it like an art director:

Refine Prompts
  • "Make slide 3 the boldest in the set, bump the headline, cut the body to one line."
  • "Swap the accent to #35C4A2 across every slide."
  • "Slide 1 hook is weak. Give me five sharper options, then apply your favourite."
  • "Tighten the spacing on slides 4 to 6 so they match slide 2."
01
Refine per slide
Claude Design edits one element without touching the rest, work slide by slide.
02
Check the font
Confirm your chosen typeface actually rendered. If it didn't load, it silently falls back to a system font, the number one way branded output degrades.
03
Export post-ready
Send to Canva (fully editable), or export PDF, PPTX or PNG. For Instagram: 4:5, 1080 x 1350px.
Note

Instagram allows up to 20 slides per carousel, but 6 to 10 is the sweet spot, long enough to deliver, short enough to finish. Set the count in your prompt; don't pad to fill space.

The Honest Limits

It's a genuinely good tool, but it isn't magic. Here's what to know before you rely on it, so you're not surprised halfway through a content sprint.

It's a research preview. Output quality varies and the tool is still changing. Treat first drafts as drafts, and check it before you publish.

Paid plans only. Claude Design needs Pro, Max, Team or Enterprise. There's no free-tier access, so factor that in before you build a workflow around it.

It burns usage fast. Design draws from the same usage pool as chat, Code and Cowork, and heavy sessions eat into limits quickly. Batch a week of carousels in one sitting rather than dipping in all day.

Fonts can silently fall back. It writes the CSS for your font but uses a system font if that font didn't load. Always eyeball the type after it generates.

It's a front-end, not a finisher. It's built for exploration and first drafts. The Canva and PDF handoffs exist for a reason, do final polish there.

Don't mass-produce slop. The whole promise is that it stops looking AI, and that only holds while you keep feeding it specific, referenced inputs. Lazy prompts just give you the median look, at scale.

Give it a topic. Give it a style.

Run the prompt, refine it twice, and you've got a week of content in the time it used to take to make one slide. The tool does the layout. You bring the taste: a narrow topic, a real reference, and the discipline to keep your inputs specific.
