Chapter 01: What Fable 5 Actually Is

On June 9, 2026, Anthropic released Claude Fable 5, the first model in the Claude 5 family and the first of a new "Mythos-class" tier that sits above Opus, the previous flagship line. It's the most capable model Anthropic has ever made generally available.

Fable 5 shares the same underlying model as Claude Mythos 5, a restricted version available only to approved organisations. The difference: Fable 5 adds safeguards that hand off requests in a few high-risk areas (cybersecurity, biology, chemistry) to Opus 4.8 instead. For normal work, you'll almost never notice, over 95% of sessions never trigger a fallback.

Chapter 02: The Free Window

Here's the part that matters right now. Through June 22, Fable 5 is included on Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise plans at no extra cost. From June 23, Anthropic pulls it from those plans and it moves to usage credits, with subscription access planned to return later.

On the API, it costs $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output, roughly twice the price of Opus 4.8, the previous flagship.

TipThe window is the play. You have until June 22 to run your heaviest workflows, big research jobs, long documents, multi-step agent tasks, on the strongest model available, for free. Test it on real work now so you know exactly what it's worth to you when the credits kick in.

Chapter 04: Where Fable 5 Shines

Fable 5 isn't a "use it for everything" model. It's slower and heavier than Sonnet or Opus, but on these four types of work, nothing else comes close.

01
Deeper research tasks
Hand it a research objective and it works through multiple stages on its own, searching, analysing, pressure-testing, and coming back with a finished deliverable rather than a draft. Teams review completed work instead of supervising every step.
59.0% on Humanity's Last Exam vs 41.4% (GPT-5.5) and 44.4% (Gemini 3.1 Pro)
02
Long-document analysis
The 1M-token context window means entire contracts, codebases, report packs, or months of transcripts go in at once, no chunking, no summarising in stages. It stays focused across millions of tokens and improves its output using its own notes.
1M token context, 128k output, top score on GDPval-AA knowledge work
03
Reliable agentic workflows
This is the headline upgrade: multi-step tasks without falling apart. In an agent harness like Claude Code, it plans across stages, delegates to sub-agents, and checks its own work. With file-based memory it reached the final act of a long strategy game three times more often than Opus 4.8.
85.0% on OSWorld-Verified computer use, best of any frontier model
04
Big coding work & vibe coding
Large migrations, complex builds, multi-day autonomous sessions. It writes its own tests, implements designs with high fidelity, and uses vision to check outputs against goals. Cursor's CEO called it the state-of-the-art model on CursorBench.

Chapter 05: Setup Guide

There's nothing to install. If you're on the right plan, Fable 5 is already waiting in your model picker. Two minutes, start to finish.

1
Check your plan
Fable 5 is included free on Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise through June 22. From June 23 it requires usage credits.
2
Select it in claude.ai
Open claude.ai (or the Claude app), click the model picker at the top of a chat, and choose Claude Fable 5. That's the whole setup.
3
Set effort high and let it think
Anthropic's own guidance: default to high effort. Fable 5 reflects on and validates its own work before answering, the extra thinking is what makes the autonomy reliable. Don't rush it.
4
Using Claude Code? Switch models
Run the /model command in Claude Code and select Fable 5. Point it at your biggest task, migrations, refactors, full builds, not quick edits.
5
Building on the API?
The model string is claude-fable-5. Pricing is $10 / $50 per million input/output tokens, with a 1M context window and up to 128k output tokens.
NoteIf a request touches a restricted area (cybersecurity, biology, chemistry), Fable 5 automatically hands it to Opus 4.8 and tells you it has. For everyday research, coding, content, and analysis work you will almost certainly never see this happen.

Chapter 06: Prompt It Differently

The biggest shift early users report: stop giving Fable 5 tasks and start giving it objectives. It plans, breaks work into stages, and checks itself, so the prompt that works is the one that describes the outcome, not the steps.

Instead of this:

"Summarise this report."

Give it this:

"Here are 12 months of our analytics exports. Find the
three biggest growth opportunities, pressure-test each
one against the data, and give me a one-page
recommendation I could action this week."

Let it run: Long, multi-stage responses are the point, not a bug. Send the objective, let it finish all stages, then review. Interrupting mid-flight wastes the model's biggest strength.

Rewrite your old instructions: Anthropic advises rewriting old prompt scaffolding written for weaker models, heavy step-by-step hand-holding now gets in the way. Give Fable 5 more judgment, not less.

When NOT to use it: Quick lookups, short edits, simple drafts, one-line code fixes. Fable 5 is slower and token-hungry, early testers describe it as best reserved for heavy jobs. Use Sonnet or Haiku for the quick stuff and save Fable for work that earns it.

TipA simple filter: if the task would take you more than an hour, it's a Fable 5 job. If it takes under a minute, it isn't.
