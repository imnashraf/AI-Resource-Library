Chapter 01, What to Know

A playbook exists so you stop improvising. Before you write a single line, you need to be clear on three things: who you're for, what they're struggling with, and why you're worth their thirty seconds. Most cold outreach fails right here. Not in the writing, but in the aim. A brilliant message sent to the wrong person still gets deleted.

Define your ideal client first

You can't write a message that lands until you know exactly who it's landing on. Vague targeting produces vague emails, and vague emails get ignored. Get specific, then build everything around that one person.

Build your ICP: nail these five

  • Industry & niche: the narrower the better. "Boutique fitness studios" beats "businesses".
  • Company size: a solo founder and a 200-person team have completely different problems.
  • Decision-maker: who actually signs off, named by role, not job title soup.
  • The pain: the one specific problem they'd happily pay to make disappear.
  • The trigger: what recently changed that makes now the moment to reach out.
TIP: USE CLAUDE

Paste a prospect's website, a recent post, or a funding announcement into Claude and ask it to pull out the three problems they're most likely facing right now. You'll write a sharper, more relevant opener in seconds.

Chapter 01, The Big Idea: Context Beats Flattery

Most people think personalisation is the answer. It isn't. Not on its own.

Personalisation is about who someone is: "Loved your talk at the conference." It's flattering, but it rarely connects to a problem they'd pay to solve.

Contextualisation is about what's happening around them right now: "Saw you just opened a second location. Staffing two sites is a different game." That creates instant relevance. It tells them you understand their situation, not just their name.

Personalisation: Weak Alone
"I saw you studied at the same university as me."
Focuses on who they are. Flatters the ego, but doesn't touch a real problem.
Contextualisation: What Works
"Noticed you just launched on three new channels. Tracking them all is usually where it breaks."
Focuses on what's changing for them. Creates relevance, urgency, and a clear reason to write back.
TIP

A trigger event is the single best reason to reach out: a launch, a new hire, a funding round, a tool they just adopted, or a post complaining about a problem. Lead with the trigger, not with yourself. That is what turns a cold email into a timely one.

Get this right and the rest of the playbook gets easier. Everything that follows (the subject line, the hook, the follow-ups) is just a way of carrying that context to the right person at the right moment.

Chapter 02, What to Say

This is where outreach is won or lost. The goal of a cold email is not to close. It's to earn a single reply. Keep it short. Under 150 words. Every extra sentence is another reason to stop reading.

NOTE: BEFORE YOU SEND

Sort your email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) so you actually land in the inbox, not spam. And stop fixating on open rates. They're unreliable and easy to misread. The metric that matters is replies, and replies come from emails worth reading.

The first email: six parts

1
The subject line
Everything depends on it. If it isn't opened, nothing else matters. Spark curiosity tied to their world, not yours. Specific and intriguing beats clever.
2
The open
Who you are, why you're credible. Fast. No long wind-up.
3
The hook
"This is about you." Researched. A specific observation about their situation that's impossible to skim past.
4
The value
One clear benefit framed around their problem. Not your feature list.
5
The proof
Others like them already trust you. No name-dropping competitors. Just enough to build belief.
6
The ask
Low-barrier question. Not a meeting. "Would it help if I showed you how?" A single positive reply is the only goal.

Chapter 02, Example: What It Looks Like Put Together

Here are the six parts assembled into one short email. Notice how every line earns the next, and the ask costs the reader almost nothing.

Cold email, Under 150 words

Subject: Quick idea for [their recent launch]

Hi [name],

I run [what you do]. We help [their type of business] turn [problem] into [outcome].

Saw you just launched [thing]. Most teams at that stage quietly lose [specific costly problem] without realising it.

A few [similar businesses] switched to us for exactly this and saw [result] in [timeframe].

Would it help if I sent over how we'd approach it for you?

[your name]
TIP: USE CLAUDE

Drafted something too long or too salesy? Paste it into Claude and ask it to cut it under 150 words while keeping the hook and the low-barrier ask. Then ask for two subject-line variants and send the one with more intrigue.

Read it out loud before you send. If a line doesn't earn its place, cut it. Short isn't a style choice here. It's what protects your reply rate.

Chapter 03, The Sequence

One email rarely does it. Most of the replies come from the follow-ups. But only if each one adds something. Never just "bumping this." Every touch earns its place. Space them out, stay consistent, and let belief build.

1 Email one: the opener
Short, contextual, one low-barrier ask. The only goal is a positive reply. Don't pitch a meeting here.

2 Email two: the value drop
Once they've replied, you're clear to share more. Send a short case study, a quick video, or a custom page built for them. An image that links to a tailored page often beats a plain link. It earns the click.

3 Email three: the proof
Show, don't tell. A screenshot or a piece of sample data that makes the opportunity real for them. Seeing it lands harder than reading about it.

4 Email four: the nudge
Bring a fresh angle: how someone similar is using it, a relevant result, a reason it's worth a conversation now. This is where you can finally move toward a call.

NOTE

Don't ask for a meeting in email one or two. Earn the reply first, build belief, then ask. Pushing for a call too early is the fastest way to get ignored for good.

Chapter 04, What to Show

Words get you the reply. Proof gets you the call. This is the collateral you reach for once someone's paying attention. These are the assets that turn "maybe" into "let's talk." Have them ready before you start, so a warm reply never goes cold while you scramble.

Sample data & screenshots

A glimpse of the actual insight or result, specific to them. The most convincing thing you can send, because it's real and not a promise.

Case studies

A short story of a similar client and the outcome you got them. Same situation, proven result. Keep it to one tight paragraph.

A custom landing page

A simple page built for one prospect, showing exactly what they need to see. Link it from an image in the email. Curiosity earns the click better than a bare URL.

A short video

A 60-second walkthrough. Record one quickly. It explains the offer better than a wall of text ever could.

Testimonials & social proof

Evidence that other people already made this decision and were glad they did. Borrowed confidence, when yours is still unproven to them.

TIP

Match the asset to the moment. Early on, a single screenshot or a one-line result does more than a ten-page deck. Save the heavy collateral for when they actually ask for it.

Chapter 05, What to Do

Knowing and saying mean nothing without doing. This is the operating side of the playbook: where you reach, how often, and how you turn a warm reply into a booked call.

Pick the right channel

Email is the backbone for personalised, considered outreach. LinkedIn works when your buyer lives there. It is ideal for B2B decision-makers. A call suits a direct, high-intent approach. Don't spread thin: go deep where your client actually is, then layer a second channel to stay top of mind.

Follow up with discipline

Most replies arrive after the first email, not from it. Space your follow-ups. Keep them consistent, never annoying. Make every one carry something new. The moment a follow-up is just "any update?", you've lost the thread.

Close the loop

Once there's genuine interest, stop selling and start scheduling. Be clear about the next step and propose a specific time. A gentle, honest reason to act now (a slot opening, a real window) helps, but only if it's true. Make saying yes the easiest thing in the email.

TIP

Go multichannel once a lead is warm. A LinkedIn message after an email, a light touch of retargeting. Stay visible without being a pest. Presence beats persistence.

Chapter 06, The Whole Playbook, One Page

Run this before every campaign. If you can tick all of it, you're ready to send.

What to Know
  • One specific ideal client
  • A real reason to reach out now
  • Context about their world
What to Say
  • Email under 150 words
  • Curiosity subject line
  • Researched hook
  • One benefit + light proof + low-barrier ask
  • Authentication sorted
What to Show
  • Case study/sample data/video ready
  • Custom page or screenshot for warm leads
What to Do
  • Right channel
  • Spaced value-adding follow-up sequence
  • Clear next step

One sharp message to the right person beats a thousand generic ones. Aim, don't spray.

Now Go Win the Reply.

Cold outreach isn't a numbers game played with the same bad email a thousand times. It's one sharp message, sent to the right person, at the right moment, followed up with discipline. Pick one ideal client. Write one email under 150 words. Lead with context. Earn the reply, then earn the call. That's the whole playbook.
