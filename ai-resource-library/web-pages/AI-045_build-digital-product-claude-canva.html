Chapter 01: The Opportunity

A digital product is anything someone can buy, download, and use without you shipping a physical thing. A meal plan. A workout guide. A set of templates. You make it once and sell it again and again.

The hard part was always the making. Writing the content took days. Designing it took skills most people don't have. That is the exact bottleneck Claude and Canva remove. Claude writes and structures the content. Canva turns it into something that looks professional. Together they collapse a week of work into an evening.

This guide walks through the four steps, in order. Read it once, then do it with a real product idea open in front of you.

Chapter 02: Step 1 - Write The Prompt

The prompt is everything. Before you open Canva or Claude, you need to know exactly what product you're making and who it's for. Get the prompt right and Claude hands you a finished draft. Get it vague and you'll spend an hour pulling teeth.

Use this structure every time:

THE STRUCTURE

Create a [product type] for [specific audience]
who want to [specific outcome]. Include [number]
pages/sections. Keep the tone [casual / professional
/ motivational]. Format it so it's ready to design
in Canva.

A real example:

EXAMPLE PROMPT

Create a 7-day meal plan for busy mums who want
to eat healthier without spending hours cooking.
Include a shopping list at the end. Keep the tone
friendly and practical. Format it so it's ready to
design in Canva.

That one prompt gives you all the content you need before you've opened Canva once. Notice what makes it work: a specific audience, a specific outcome, a set length, a defined tone, and the instruction to format for design.

TipThe narrower the audience, the better the product. 'For busy mums' beats 'for people' every time. A tight audience makes the content feel written for one person.

Chapter 03: Step 2 - Structure It For Canva

Once you've got the content back, ask Claude to format it clearly so it maps directly onto Canva pages. This is the step most people skip, and it's the one that saves you hours of cutting and reorganising content inside Canva later.

Paste this straight after your first prompt:

FORMATTING PROMPT

Now break this into individual pages. Each page
should have a title, a short paragraph, and no more
than five bullet points. Keep it clean and easy to
lay out.

Now every page Claude gives you has the same shape: one title, one short paragraph, and a handful of bullets. That consistency is what lets you fly through the design step. You're no longer reading a wall of text and deciding what goes where. You're copying page one into page one.

Why this matters:

  • Predictable layout. Every page has identical structure, so one Canva template fits all of them.
  • No editing in Canva. Canva is a slow place to write. Do all the writing in Claude where editing is fast.
  • Faster than copy-paste. You're matching shape to shape, not hunting for where each sentence belongs.

Chapter 04: Step 3 - Build It In Canva

You've got clean, page-shaped content. Now you turn it into a finished design. There are two ways to do this. Start with the first. Move to the second when you want to skip tabs entirely.

Option A: Drop it into a template

1. Pick a template that matches your product: Open Canva and search for your product type directly. Meal plan, ebook, guide, worksheet. Canva has thousands of pre-designed templates for each.

TipSearch the exact product name. 'Meal plan' returns better results than 'document'.

2. Go page by page and replace the placeholder text: You're not designing from scratch. You're swapping the template's dummy text for your actual content. Title into title, paragraph into paragraph, bullets into bullets. Because your content is already page-shaped, it slots in cleanly.

TipResist restyling. The template already looks good. Just change the words.

Option B: The native Canva connector

Claude has a native Canva connector. Once enabled, you can prompt Claude to generate and edit Canva designs directly, without switching between tabs. You stay in one conversation and the design appears in your Canva account.

1. Enable the Canva connector in Claude: In Claude, go to Settings and open Connectors. Find Canva and follow the prompts to connect your account. It's a standard sign-in, the same as connecting any other app.

TipOne-time setup. Once connected it stays available across your chats.

2. Turn it on for your chat: Start a new conversation, open the tools menu, and switch Canva on for that session. Now Claude can act inside Canva when you ask it to.

3. Prompt Claude to build the design: Ask in plain English. Claude can create new designs, autofill templates with your content, search your existing designs, and resize or export them. Your structured pages from Step 2 become the content it fills in.

TipBe specific: name the product, the style, and the page count in your prompt.

Chapter 05: Step 4 - The Reusable Formula

Save this and reuse it every time. Just swap out the brackets. This is the same structure from Step 1, tightened into one skeleton you can paste for any product you'll ever make.

THE FORMULA - PASTE AND SWAP THE BRACKETS

Create a [product type] for [audience] who want to
[outcome]. Make it [number] pages. Tone should be
[tone]. Structure each page with a title, a short
intro, and bullet points. Format it ready to drop
into Canva.

How to fill each bracket:

  • [product type]: The format. Meal plan, workout guide, ebook, planner, template pack.
  • [audience]: Who it's for, as narrow as you can make it. Busy mums, new gym-goers, freelancers.
  • [outcome]: The result they want. Eat healthier, build muscle, get organised.
  • [number]: How many pages or sections. Keep first products short: five to ten pages.
  • [tone]: How it should read. Friendly, professional, motivational, no-nonsense.
TipKeep this formula in a note on your phone. The moment a product idea lands, you can run it in under a minute.
